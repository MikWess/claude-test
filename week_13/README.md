## Learning Objectives

By the end of this week, you should be able to:

---

## AP CSP Concepts

### IOC-1: Computing Innovations
**Computing innovations** include physical devices, software, and computing concepts.

Examples:
- Social media platforms
- GPS navigation
- Machine learning/AI
- Cryptocurrencies
- Autonomous vehicles

### Beneficial and Harmful Effects
Most innovations have BOTH positive and negative effects.

| Innovation | Benefits | Concerns |
|------------|----------|----------|
| **Social Media** | Connect people globally | Misinformation, addiction |
| **AI/Machine Learning** | Automation, efficiency | Job displacement, bias |
| **GPS** | Navigation, safety | Privacy tracking |
| **Online Shopping** | Convenience, selection | Local business impact |

### IOC-2: Digital Divide
The **digital divide** is the gap between those with and without access to computing.

**Factors:**
- Geographic location (rural vs urban)
- Economic status
- Age and education
- Infrastructure availability

**Consequences:**
- Educational disadvantages
- Limited job opportunities
- Reduced access to services

### IOC-2: Privacy and Security

**Personally Identifiable Information (PII):**
- Name, address, phone number
- Social security number
- Biometric data
- Location data

**Privacy Concerns:**
- Data collection by companies
- Tracking and surveillance
- Data breaches
- Third-party data sharing

**Security Concepts:**
- **Encryption**: Scrambling data so only authorized parties can read it
- **Phishing**: Tricking users into revealing information
- **Malware**: Malicious software (viruses, ransomware)
- **Strong Passwords**: Mix of characters, length, uniqueness

### IOC-1: Intellectual Property
- **Copyright**: Automatic protection for creative works
- **Open Source**: Software with publicly available source code
- **Creative Commons**: Licenses for sharing work with conditions
- **Plagiarism**: Using others' work without attribution

### IOC-2: Bias in Computing
Algorithms can reflect or amplify human biases:
- Biased training data → biased AI
- Facial recognition accuracy varies by demographic
- Search algorithms can reinforce stereotypes
- Hiring algorithms may discriminate

### Legal and Ethical Concerns
- **DMCA**: Digital Millennium Copyright Act
- **CFAA**: Computer Fraud and Abuse Act
- **COPPA**: Children's Online Privacy Protection
- **GDPR**: European data protection regulation

## AP-Style Practice Questions

### Question 1
A company uses an AI to screen job applications. The AI was trained on data from the past 10 years, when most employees were male. What is the most likely effect?
- A) The AI will hire only qualified candidates
- B) The AI may unfairly rank female applicants lower
- C) The AI will not function correctly
- D) The AI will hire more diverse candidates

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
B) The AI may unfairly rank female applicants lower — AI trained on biased data learns that bias
</details>

### Question 2
Which is an example of PII (Personally Identifiable Information)?
- A) A person's favorite color
- B) A person's social security number
- C) The current weather
- D) A publicly traded stock price

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
B) A person's social security number — PII can identify a specific individual
</details>

### Question 3
A small town has limited broadband internet access. This is an example of:
- A) The digital divide
- B) A security breach
- C) Copyright infringement
- D) Algorithm bias

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
A) The digital divide — Limited access to computing resources
</details>

### Question 4
Using HTTPS instead of HTTP provides:
- A) Faster loading times
- B) Encryption of data in transit
- C) More storage space
- D) Better graphics

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
B) Encryption of data in transit — HTTPS encrypts communication between browser and server
</details>

### Question 5
A student copies code from the internet and submits it as their own work. This is an example of:
- A) Open source development
- B) Plagiarism
- C) Collaboration
- D) Crowdsourcing

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
B) Plagiarism — Using others' work without attribution
</details>

## Key Takeaways

1. Computing innovations have both benefits and harms
2. The digital divide affects access to opportunities
3. Protecting PII is crucial for privacy
4. Algorithms can perpetuate human biases
5. Ethical computing requires considering impacts on all users
